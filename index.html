Notes

How the score works

Start at 10 and subtract for wind noise, precipitation noise, wind-driven leaf rustle and the insect noise you select. Calm, dry conditions with little local noise can reach 10. Several noise sources together can reach 0. Each night averages the unrounded hourly scores and rounds once to the nearest half point.

9–10 Excellent · 8–8.5 Great · 7–7.5 Good · 6–6.5 Fair · 5–5.5 Marginal · 3–4.5 Poor · 0–2.5 Bad.

These noise sources can mask calls. Their point values are practical planning rules, not a validated measure of clarity, detection probability or microphone performance. Wind protection, shelter, bird distance and equipment also matter. A low score does not mean recording is useless.

Wind and gusts use the weather model’s 10 m height. Microphone height is site information only. Precipitation is water equivalent, not a measurement of rain noise; snow and rain can affect recordings differently. Missing wind, gust or precipitation data prevent a nightly rating. User-selected foliage and insect conditions apply to all displayed nights, including historical nights.

Temperature, humidity, visibility, clouds and pressure do not affect the score. No bird altitude, bending, sound loss in dB, best recording window or spectrogram detail is predicted. The experimental propagation model remains archived.

Completed-night precipitation retains the separate 18:00–06:00 local estimate shared with NFC Tools. Optional roads and airports are map context only and do not affect the score.
